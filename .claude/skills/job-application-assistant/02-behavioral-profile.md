# Behavioral Profile

## Overview
Andrea's behavioral profile combines strong planning and explanatory instincts with a need for team connection, regular feedback, and visible progress milestones. He does his best work when scope is clear upfront, outcomes are tangible, and he has people around him to think with and learn from. Works well in structured teams with kind, direct, technically strong managers.

*No formal assessment completed. Based on self-reported behavioral interview, June 2026.*

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Structure / planning | High | Needs to understand full scope and design the workflow before executing |
| Team connection | High | Loses focus in isolation; energised by collaboration and regular feedback |
| Teaching / explaining | High | Explaining and organising knowledge is genuinely energising, not a burden |
| Feedback loops | High | Long, opaque timelines cause restarts; short visible milestones sustain momentum |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Planner and explainer:** Thrives when he can map out a workflow, understand the full picture upfront, and share that understanding with others — teaching is a strength, not a side task
- **Visual and spatial thinker:** Works best thinking out loud on a whiteboard; processes problems through drawing and spatial reasoning
- **Team-oriented technician:** Despite working mostly alone in research, strongly prefers team environments with regular check-ins — isolation without feedback is a meaningful drain
- **Milestone-driven executor:** Starts enthusiastically with a clear idea; loses momentum when outcomes are distant or unclear. Shorter feedback loops and well-defined deliverables sustain his focus through implementation

## How You Work Best
- Clear scope and deliverables defined before starting — planning time upfront is not wasted, it's essential
- Regular check-ins and feedback from colleagues or manager (not micromanagement, but not silence either)
- Team environment even if the technical work is individually owned — knowing others are present and engaged helps
- Sprint-based or milestone-driven delivery: visible progress at regular intervals
- Whiteboard-friendly culture where thinking is done out loud and collaboratively
- A technically strong manager from whom something can be learned — not just an administrator

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Long opaque timelines:** "I work best when I can break a large problem into clear milestones. I've developed habits around upfront scoping and incremental validation — I don't just dive in."
- **Transitioning from solo research to team industry work:** "My research context was largely independent, and I'm actively looking for a role where I can collaborate more closely. That's part of what draws me to this type of position."
- **Sustaining through uncertain middle phases:** "I've learned to manage this by front-loading planning and checking in regularly — external structure (sprint reviews, defined deliverables) makes a real difference for me."

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "collaborative team", "close-knit team", "regular standups or reviews"
- "structured delivery", "sprint-based", "clear milestones"
- "mentorship", "learning culture", "technically strong team"
- "knowledge sharing", "documentation culture", "cross-functional collaboration"
- "defined scope", "clear deliverables", "agile"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker, but worth probing at interview):
- "fast-paced and constantly changing priorities" — short loops are fine, chaotic pivots are not
- "highly autonomous, you'll figure it out" — autonomy without feedback is draining
- "startup, wear many hats" — depends on whether there's still structure and a team
- "long-term open-ended research" — fine if milestones and check-ins are built in

## Management Style Preferences
- **Kind and direct:** Delivers honest feedback without harshness — no ambiguity about where things stand
- **Technically strong:** Someone Andrea can learn from, not just be managed by
- **Present and responsive:** Regular enough contact that Andrea doesn't feel adrift; doesn't need to be daily but should be predictable
- **Gives clear scope:** Sets expectations upfront and doesn't move the goalposts mid-task

## Long-Term Career Aspiration
Andrea wants to become a recognised in-house expert within a company over time, growing into a support/mentor role for colleagues rather than staying purely an individual contributor. Frame first-role applications as the foundation for this trajectory: emphasise knowledge-sharing, documentation, and teaching instincts already present (see "Planner and explainer" above) as early evidence of mentoring capability, without overstating current seniority.

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** Emphasise planning and workflow design ("I invest in understanding the problem before writing code"), team collaboration, and knowledge-sharing
- **CV:** Highlight end-to-end workflow ownership and documentation — these demonstrate structured thinking
- **Interviews:** Use STAR examples that show upfront planning, collaborative problem-solving, and teaching/explaining moments (e.g. cross-institutional collaboration, conference presentations)
- **Don't overstate:** Do not claim high comfort with full ambiguity, very long solo projects without check-ins, or purely administrative management
